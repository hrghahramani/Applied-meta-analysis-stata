
# Applied Meta-Analysis in Stata: Effect Sizes, Heterogeneity & Meta-Regression

## Overview

This repository documents practical work completed during an educational meta-analysis training project using Stata. The project focused on effect size estimation, forest plot interpretation, heterogeneity assessment, outlier detection, sensitivity analysis, subgroup analysis, meta-regression, and publication bias assessment.

A major practical component involved a dataset on deep brain stimulation (DBS) targets in epilepsy, providing hands-on experience with meta-analytic workflows and statistical interpretation.

The repository demonstrates practical skills in statistical analysis and evidence synthesis. It is intended for educational and professional development purposes.

## Project Objectives

- Prepare and clean research datasets for meta-analysis.
- Select and interpret appropriate effect size measures.
- Generate and interpret forest plots.
- Assess between-study heterogeneity using multiple approaches.
- Identify potential outliers and evaluate their influence on pooled estimates.
- Conduct leave-one-out sensitivity analysis.
- Explore sources of heterogeneity through subgroup analysis and meta-regression.
- Assess potential publication bias using statistical methods.

---

## Task 01 — Meta-Analysis Foundations & Study Selection

### Objectives

Develop an understanding of the fundamental principles of meta-analysis and evidence synthesis.

### Methods & Topics

- Study eligibility and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Mean Difference (MD) and Standardized Mean Difference (SMD).
- Odds Ratio (OR), Risk Ratio (RR), and Risk Difference (RD).
- Interpretation of reported effect sizes.
- Diagnostic accuracy concepts, including sensitivity and specificity.

## Task 02 — Data Cleaning & Effect Size Estimation

### Objectives

Prepare a dataset on deep brain stimulation targets in epilepsy for meta-analytic analysis in Stata.

### Methods

- Cleaning categorical variables, including epilepsy type and study quality.
- Importing and organizing data in Stata.
- Estimating effect sizes.
- Generating and interpreting forest plots.
- Examining pooled effects and statistical significance.

## Task 03 — Heterogeneity Assessment

### Objectives

Evaluate between-study variability and its implications for pooled results.

### Methods

- Assessing heterogeneity using multiple methods covered in training.
- Interpreting heterogeneity statistics and forest plot patterns.
- Evaluating variation across study-level effect estimates.
- Exploring potential sources of heterogeneity.

## Task 04 — Outlier Detection & Sensitivity Analysis

### Objectives

Investigate potentially influential studies and assess the robustness of meta-analysis results.

### Methods

- Identifying potential outliers.
- Generating and interpreting Galbraith plots.
- Conducting leave-one-out sensitivity analysis.
- Comparing forest plots and pooled estimates with and without suspected outliers.

## Task 05 — Subgroup Analysis, Meta-Regression & Publication Bias

### Objectives

Explore potential moderators of heterogeneity and assess possible publication bias.

### Methods

- Subgroup analysis by epilepsy type.
- Subgroup analysis by age group (≤40 and >40 years).
- Meta-regression using age-related variables.
- Publication bias assessment using the Doleman et al. approach.
- Trim-and-fill analysis and interpretation of findings.

## Additional Practical Exercise — Forest Plot Interpretation & Data Extraction

- Interpreting study-level effect estimates, confidence intervals, study weights, and pooled estimates.
- Exploring potential outliers and interpreting high heterogeneity.
- Extracting data points from plotted curves and scatter plots using plot digitization tools.

This exercise used a published umbrella review on COVID-19 vaccine hesitancy as a source for forest plot interpretation.
